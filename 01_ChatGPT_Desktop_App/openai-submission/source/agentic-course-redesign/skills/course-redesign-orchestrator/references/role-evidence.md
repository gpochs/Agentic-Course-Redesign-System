# Evidence for redesign decisions

Use the section for the role and decision at hand. The orchestrator includes its relevant question and source in the specialist's brief. Read new research for the lecturer's discipline and learner population when the run permits it. These starting points were checked against primary or institutional sources on 27 November 2026. Distinguish a measured learning effect, a design framework, an institutional practice and a product capability. Record who participated, the comparison, the measure and when it was taken before transferring a result to another setting.

## Orchestrator

If a role cannot make a required observation with its approved methods, consult the [role-specific evidence and tool checks](tool-fit.md). Use public, task-only terms and tell the teacher what the extra tool would change. The orchestrator owns the decision and its consent record.

Before each stage and specialist dispatch, apply the [model plan](model-plan.md). Supply the bounded task, model or inherited route and required capability. Record independent execution evidence, or mark the model unverified.

**Research.** Ask what learners should be able to do. Preserve their lecturer's wording and identify which claim each of the five preliminary roles can check. Separate observed course features from proposals and from unanswered questions. Parallelise independent evidence gathering within the host limit. Keep a single owner of the decision record and live browser tab. Relay a disagreement with its sources and the decision it changes. A majority of agents repeating the same source contributes one line of evidence.

**Plan.** At HITL 1 let the lecturer choose the research focus. At HITL 2 present a genuine alternative, its evidence, workload and uncertainty. Following selection, specify the student action, the evidence of learning, the target artefact, and how the lecturer will recognise success at Gate 3. Carry forward approved decisions once. Reopen a decision if a new constraint changes it.

**Implement.** At each named production gate use the approved target and audience. Show the lecturer the resulting student view or editable preview. Record what was opened, which task actually worked, and any limitation. After independent QA, retain the production declaration, handoff and HITL 3. Research–Plan–Implement is an adaptation of a software-engineering practice. Learner outcomes require evidence from the educational setting. [HumanLayer's original practice account](https://www.hlyr.dev/blog/advanced-context-engineering) · [Current HumanLayer workflow](https://docs.humanlayer.com/reference/skills-workflows)

Cemri and colleagues found failures of specification, coordination and verification in multi-agent benchmarks. Use distinct deliverables and check the combined result against the original task. Anthropic's engineering account suggests bounded briefs and end-state evaluation for open-ended research. Its results concern its own system. OpenAI documents model inheritance and parent-session permissions in Codex. Use the lecturer's available model by default. Where the host supports selection, Sol is an option for well-specified independent work and Astra for difficult evidence reconciliation or complex production. These are routing choices. Verify the same deliverable-level criteria on either model. [Cemri et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b1041e52d3be19f0a9bc491657488e4a-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html) · [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multi-agent-research-system) · [OpenAI subagents](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/agent-configuration/subagents) · [OpenAI model release](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/)

**Gaps and capabilities.** For every role, tie a missing source, capability or contextual fact to a decision it changes. Supply a minimal request and a feasible fallback to the orchestrator. A missing rubric may limit the evidence for alignment, while a missing learner-preview permission may limit feedback-timing QA. Neither makes the rest of the redesign wait.

### Asking about course context

Choose the next question from the decision in front of the lecturer. When learners have different starting points, identify a brief, low-stakes task that would show what they already know. CAST's UDL Guidelines 3.0 describe ways to connect prior knowledge to new learning. This is guidance for designing options. Check what works with the particular learners and activity. [CAST guidance on prior knowledge](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/building-knowledge/prior-knowledge/)

Use the local source that defines the course. As Swiss examples, UZH's planning guidance points students to faculty information and its course catalogue for content, duration and credits. The EDK describes how frameworks and cantonal curricula relate in Swiss schooling. For an individual adjustment the responsible institution determines the process. UZH's Disability Office explains its university procedure. Canton Zürich publishes a separate procedure for upper-secondary schools. In another setting look up that setting's current rules only with approved access. Give specialists the teaching or assessment constraint they need, in non-identifying terms. [UZH study planning](https://www.students.uzh.ch/de/new/planning/modulebooking.html) · [EDK curriculum guidance](https://www.edk.ch/de/bildungssystem/beschreibung/lehrplaene) · [UZH accommodation FAQ](https://www.disabilityoffice.uzh.ch/de/FAQ.html) · [Canton Zürich upper-secondary guidance](https://www.zh.ch/de/bildung/bildungssystem/chancengerechtigkeit/nachteilsausgleich-sek-ii.html)

**Fictional school example, after Skip.**

Lecturer: “My first-year Gymnasium class can label a climate graph. I want them to explain what the pattern means.”

Orchestrator: “What have they already tried when an explanation calls for evidence from the graph?”

Lecturer: “I'm unsure. We have ten minutes and some pupils need a text version.”

Orchestrator: “We can use the first minute to find out. Give the class the same values in a graph and a labelled table. Ask learners to describe one pattern, then support it with two values. Their first answers will tell us whether to practise reading values or explaining the pattern. Which would you like to focus on first?”

**Fictional university example, after approved source review.**

Orchestrator: “Your module description fixes a written exam and asks students to justify a method. The lab sheet already gives them practice running it. How much freedom do you have to change the practice before the exam?”

Lecturer: “I can change the seminar, including feedback. Keep the exam as it is. I can't share anyone's individual arrangements.”

Orchestrator: “That gives us enough. In the seminar, students could choose between two methods and defend their choice. Then they could revise one sentence after seeing an alternative justification. We can offer the practice case in accessible text for everyone and keep the written exam criteria. What would distinguish a sound justification in your field?”

In both conversations the lecturer sees one decision at a time. The specialist report can carry a consequential uncertainty to the next gate without repeating the opening questions.

## Course Mapper

Trace an outcome from its published wording to the first opportunity to learn it, a rehearsal, feedback, and the performance sampled in assessment. Record an exact source location, or mark a gap in the available evidence. Compare the intellectual demand at each point. A lecture and a multiple-choice recall item on the same topic do not by themselves show that students practised or demonstrated a promised explanation. Ronald Harden's curriculum-mapping guide originates in medical education. John Biggs supplies the broader higher-education alignment argument. Both offer planning methods, rather than course-specific proof of effectiveness. [Harden, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1080/01421590120036547) · [Biggs, 1996](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00138871) · [Monash University design guide](https://www.monash.edu/learning-teaching/teachhq/Teaching-practices/learning-outcomes/how-to/constructive-alignment)

For a programme-wide brief, identify which course tasks actually sample a shared programme outcome. Yang Xu's management-programme case describes mapping programme goals from course-level evidence. Use it to organise the map and document any missing course or assessment evidence. A single programme case establishes no general effect on learning. [Xu, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijme.2025.101315)

## Active Learning Researcher

For each option, specify what students will *do and produce*, who will respond and what students can revise. For example, ask learners to predict individually, explain a discrepancy with a peer and submit a revised explanation. Provide an equally purposeful individual or asynchronous route if collaboration is a barrier. Chi and Wylie's ICAP framework differentiates physical activity, generating ideas and jointly developing them. Use the actual contribution as evidence of the mode. Yang and colleagues' classroom meta-analysis supports retrieval practice on average, with effects varying across conditions including feedback and test format. Choose a new application or delayed check when the intended outcome is transfer or retention. [Chi and Wylie, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823) · [Yang et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309)

If the goal is transfer, specify an unfamiliar application and what a successful response would show. Ruitenburg and colleagues' 2026 meta-analysis found an average retrieval advantage in primary and secondary classrooms, with effects varying by outcome and no significant pooled transfer effect in that subgroup. The Learning Designer and Red Team can use this distinction to check whether a proposed quiz rehearses recall or tests the promised application. [Ruitenburg et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10225-5)

## AI Integration Researcher

Choose the capability the learner must retain. Specify what AI may do, when the learner must attempt or judge an answer, how feedback is checked and where comparable independent performance will be observed. Include an accessible alternative and a human help route. In secondary mathematics, Bastani and colleagues found improved assisted practice with both interfaces and poorer subsequent unassisted performance in the general-interface condition. The guarded tutor mitigated that penalty. In two undergraduate physics lessons, Kestin and colleagues' purpose-built tutor improved immediate post-test scores relative to an active class. These studies evaluate different interventions, learners and outcomes. Neither establishes the effect of an untested chatbot in this course. [Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) · [Publisher's affiliation correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12403119/) · [Kestin et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6)

When the proposed benefit is engagement, track an observable action such as revising a solution after a hint, alongside independent learning evidence. Ennion and McLellan's 2026 experiment with 16–18-year-olds found different patterns of effort, perceived difficulty and attempts across human tutoring, AI tutoring and search. Challenge-seeking did not differ by condition. Do not infer mastery from chat length or self-reported effort. [Ennion and McLellan, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70100)

## Student Experience Critic

Walk the student-facing route from finding the task to submitting, receiving feedback and knowing the next step. Identify specific obstacles and uncertainty. Estimate workload as a range with explicit assumptions about reading purpose, prior knowledge, practice, tool setup and revision. Rice University's calculator documents assumptions behind its estimates. A Harvard physics experiment found perceptions of learning and post-test performance could diverge. Treat time estimates, learner perceptions, measured performance and proxy judgements as different evidence. [Rice University workload estimator](https://cte.rice.edu/resources/workload-estimator) · [Deslauriers et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116)

Include technology handoffs, access to alternatives and the timing of accommodations. Simon Peter Ngalomba's qualitative study with 12 students and four staff with visual impairments at one Tanzanian university documents barriers across teaching materials, institutional support and assessment. Use such accounts to identify a scenario to test. Only people who actually took part can speak to their own experience. [Ngalomba, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-026-02144-y)

## Assessment and Alignment Designer

State the claim a lecturer wants to make about a student's competence. Identify what a response would show, the scoring criterion and one alternative explanation for the score, such as reading load, fluency with a tool or access to assistance. Where AI use is itself the construct, assess judgement about its output. Where independent mastery is the construct, specify how that evidence is obtained. Try the rubric on two synthetic borderline responses and reconcile disagreements before proposing it. Kane's validity framework evaluates interpretations and uses. Panadero and colleagues found stronger evidence for a rubric's academic-performance benefit than for other outcomes. Rubric publication alone cannot establish valid scoring. [Kane, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jedm.12000) · [Panadero et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09823-4)

Consider the larger programme before adding a new high-stakes task. Lodge and colleagues' Australian expert guidance describes multiple ways to assure learning and use AI productively. Ahangama's 2026 qualitative synthesis for ICT tertiary education links competencies to assessment strategies and methods. Treat these as design options and check local regulations and actual course assessment authority. [Lodge et al., 2025, TEQSA guidance](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/enacting-assessment-reform-in-a-time-of-artificial-intelligence.pdf) · [Ahangama, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00582-0)

## Source Verification and Citation Auditor

Check a consequential claim in three passes. Resolve the bibliographic record, inspect the actual methods or passage supporting the claim, then check the publisher's update history. Quote a page, section, figure or table location. An abstract-only check supports only what the abstract reports. Search a second index or the author's institutional record when metadata disagree. A missing record warrants an unresolved flag. Reserve “fabricated” for evidence that warrants that conclusion. Check licence and permission to redistribute separately. Topaz and colleagues' 2026 study of references in five scientific and engineering fields reports false positives during expert review and explicitly limits what can be inferred about authorship. Crossmark is useful where participating publishers maintain it. [Topaz et al., 2026, university repository copy](https://www.utupub.fi/items/55624dfb-56a1-4ddc-bc60-d20a503af3ef) · [Crossref update records](https://www.crossref.org/services/crossmark/)

## Evidence and Feasibility Red Team

Pick the premise most likely to change the lecturer's decision. Attempt to disconfirm it with a contradictory study, a mismatched outcome, a missing implementation dependency, or a concrete failure on the approved target. Check the least-resourced route, including time for feedback and support. Distinguish a demonstrated defect from a plausible risk and a question only the lecturer can resolve. Keep the useful part of a mixed result. Name the smallest test or revision that could change your verdict. The two AI trials above illustrate why an apparent contradiction can be explained by differences in comparator, population and assessment conditions. Independent end-state checks also address verification failures found in software-agent benchmarks. [Bastani et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) · [Kestin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6) · [Cemri et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b1041e52d3be19f0a9bc491657488e4a-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)

## Learning Designer

For each accepted change, design backwards from one performance, choose a practice attempt that rehearses it and include an opportunity to use feedback. Show the lecturer what learners will see or do differently, how long it will take, and what they can remove or replace to make room. For a consequential choice, show how alternatives change practice, feedback or workload. Preserve the approved outcome and grading conditions while sequencing activities. Reopen their design only if the lecturer requests it. [Monash University constructive alignment guide](https://www.monash.edu/learning-teaching/teachhq/Teaching-practices/learning-outcomes/how-to/constructive-alignment) · [Yang et al., classroom retrieval review](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309)

## Learning Material Designer

Retain the instructional function when moving between a slide deck, handout, sheet, LMS page or quiz. Segment a complex demonstration at meaningful decisions and give learners a way to pause, predict and check. Rey and colleagues found small-to-medium benefits for retention and transfer across multimedia studies, with additional learning time. Choose segmentation where the material benefits. For an editable worksheet, separate labelled inputs from examples and outputs, preserve formulas and give charts a textual equivalent. For a quiz, verify the prompt, accepted responses, boundaries, scoring and feedback timing on a synthetic attempt. [Rey et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4) · [W3C guidance for non-web documents and software](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/)

When redesigning a long demonstration, try a short segment followed by an application on a different example. Herman and colleagues found a far-transfer advantage in two small university cases using segmented instruction in a low-immersion environment. Preserve pause and navigation controls, then test the actual task on the course's delivery device. [Herman et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12528-025-09484-y)

## Artefact Accessibility and Visual QA

Perform one complete student task in the intended surface using keyboard navigation. Inspect headings or reading order, table headers, text alternatives, focus visibility, captions where applicable, and an error/feedback state. Check that equivalent routes preserve the skill assessed. Inspect the editable source and its delivered version because export may change tags, links and reading order. Use WCAG 2.2 for web content and WCAG2ICT as guidance for non-web documents and software. Record the version, scope, environment and exact outcomes of manual and automated checks. Mark assistive-technology or learner testing as unverified when unavailable. A clean checker report and a screenshot alone cannot establish conformance. [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) · [W3C WCAG2ICT](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/) · [Microsoft checker limitations](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/office-accessibility/rules-for-the-accessibility-checker) · [Rice University testing guidance](https://digitalaccessibility.rice.edu/testing)

When auditing an LMS activity, record the exact control, its accessible name, intended purpose and the outcome of keyboard activation. Adedoja's exploratory automated audit of pages at twenty US universities illustrates recurring flags involving link names, alternatives and contrast. Its alerts required human interpretation. Use them as leads for a manual task check on the actual course page. [Adedoja, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-026-01472-3)

## Helping teachers make decisions

A teacher choosing which activity to change needs a chance to explain their reasoning. Ask an open question, leave room for an answer and avoid combining several decisions. Cornell’s discussion guidance supports asking one question at a time and allowing thinking time. Using that practice in an assistant conversation is a design choice for this workflow. [Cornell Center for Teaching Innovation](https://teaching.cornell.edu/teaching-resources/engaging-students/engaging-students-discussions/facilitating-discussions)

Use short, numbered steps with meaningful titles. A single-choice control suits mutually exclusive answers. Independent source types require a genuine multi-select control or a chat answer that accepts several choices. If a lecturer may need to revise a choice, show an editable recap and offer an easy way to change it. W3C describes progress and logical groupings for multi-page forms. GOV.UK provides separate patterns for questions, radio buttons and checkboxes. Amershi and colleagues’ CHI guidelines support efficient dismissal and correction of AI suggestions. These sources inform the interaction design. They do not establish learning gains from the plugin. [W3C multi-page forms](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/multi-page/) · [GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/) · [GOV.UK radios](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/radios/) · [GOV.UK checkboxes](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/checkboxes/) · [Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233)

In an experimental creative-ideation task, Maier, Schneider and Feuerriegel found that how an AI intervened affected idea quality, diversity and perceived ownership. This was not a course-design trial. Here the practical implication is to elicit what the teacher values before suggesting a substantial rewrite, then let them inspect and change a draft. [Maier et al., CHI 2026](https://mcml.ai/publications/msf26/)

For every specialist finding, return one sentence the teacher could use: what learners currently do, a specific change and how the teacher would see whether it helped. Retain sources, uncertainties and the technical record separately. A terminology change alone is not a useful recommendation.
